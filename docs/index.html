#30DayMapChallenge · Day 8 · Utopia · Munich

Die Dachrepublik

What if the roofs of Munich's city centre belonged to everyone? Share the flat roofs among eight public uses. The simulator puts each use where people within walking distance lack it, and stops once the need is met. Roofs nobody needs stay free.

    Method and limits

    Area. Munich Stadtbezirke 1–3 (Altstadt-Lehel, Ludwigsvorstadt-Isarvorstadt, Maxvorstadt) from OpenStreetMap.

    Roofs. Bavarian LoD2 building models. Roof faces under 10° slope count as flat and can host the 7 public uses or PV; the usable net area is 75% of the flat area. Pitched faces of 10–60° facing east, south or west can carry PV.

    People. Zensus 2022, 100 m grid (residents; children are the 0–15-year-olds, the closest published group to the Excel's 0–12).

    Access. Walking distances on the OpenStreetMap path network, not straight lines, from every grid cell to the nearest existing facility (playgrounds, pitches, parks, gardens, cinemas, community centres and libraries in OSM) and to every candidate roof.

    Placement. For each use, the number of new sites is capped by the open gap from your Excel (residents ÷ people per site × (1 − already covered)) and by its share of the roof area. Sites go first to the roof that gives the most people new access within the target distance, until the gap is closed or nothing more is gained. PV fills roofs only until the kWh target per resident is met. "People gaining access" counts each person once, however many uses they gain.

    Not a building permit. These are theoretical candidates. Structure, fire safety, ownership and access are not checked. Yields and PV factors are the Excel's model assumptions (GartenLeistungen 5.45 kg/m², 0.65 × 0.2 kWp/m² × 950 kWh/kWp).
